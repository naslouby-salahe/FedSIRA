# Scientific decisions

## Maturity and candidate decision

`CURRENT_SCIENTIFIC_MATURITY = STRONG`

Candidate status: `PROMOTED`

The promoted decision is the Leave-Fault Geometric Median Certificate. The specification is `docs/algorithms/leave-fault-geometric-median-certificate.md`. A post-reference source may name the capability under investigation. Production authority is the worst leave-`f` coalition: the minimum lower-median utility and the maximum upper-median harm over every coalition that drops `f` non-source evidence domains, with the production update equal to the geometric median of those coalition means. The source production weight is identically 0.

The lifecycle remains the baseline and the experimental scaffold: clean anchor, immutable capability contract, commit-before-verifier assignment, source-excluded Krum synthesis, and a fresh final gate. Quorum, Krum, source-identity exclusion, and the final gate are baseline components. They are not the contribution. The earlier lifecycle-only stop is withdrawn.

Conformal risk control, unanimity, a geometric source-payload veto, SpectralKrum, SureFED, historical subtraction, and reputation-weighted aggregation stay rejected for the reasons in the registry below. The controlled comparison that selected the certificate is `docs/audit/pocs/leave_fault_candidates.json`. At the same fault bound, utility floor, and harm ceiling, the certificate admits the in-geometry panel without installing the malicious vector, and it admits the single-low-report panel that the lifecycle rejects. It rejects conflicting evidence and stays dormant below the configured support count.

The paired master seed remains the inferential unit for any later training comparison. The certificate's own validation experiment is the controlled-world matrix executed by `fedsira run "Leave-Fault Certificate Validation"`. The historical 1,989-cell inventory is not required for that validation.

PhD relationship: `NATURAL_EXTENSION` of collaborative malware detection with federated learning in IoT networks. The added layer is post-reference capability admission under a bounded Byzantine threat. It does not restyle earlier detector papers as this result.

## Reconstructed study

FedSIRA studies whether a federated system can safely admit a **new target-detection capability** first proposed by a potentially Byzantine participant without making that participant's model artifact the authority for production. It compares source-model approval with a path that fixes a capability contract, trains non-source reproductions from a clean anchor and local evidence, commits them before fresh verification, synthesizes only certified non-source updates with Krum when plurality survives the preregistered comparison, and applies a fresh final gate. The primary evidence is the security–utility trade-off: malicious admission/backdoor success versus legitimate target admission and clean supported-class cost.

In the primary construction, `GAFGYT_COMBO` is withheld from anchor training and validation, then introduced in target roles. Nine N-BaIoT devices are accounting units and **device-domain proxies**. Their rows are split into disjoint roles within the same device/class CSV capture. This supports a controlled cross-device replay study; it does not establish nine independent organizations, independent collection sites, or a natural post-deployment timeline. The secondary CICIoT2023 split uses nine label-conditioned hash pseudo-domains and is another synthetic partition, not independent administrative evidence.

The paper’s defensible object is the **Leave-Fault Geometric Median Certificate**: a source-independent algorithm for admitting a post-reference capability under unreliable or Byzantine evidence. The authority/admission lifecycle is the baseline. The certificate is not a reviewer vote, a privacy protocol, a generic client filter, or a production-ready IDS.

## Recommended research-question hierarchy

### Primary

Under the declared one-Byzantine-reproducer/one-Byzantine-verifier profile and device-proxy data construction, does source-excluded reconstruction with a fresh final gate reduce compromised production admission while retaining the legitimate target capability, compared with the strongest simple source-review and independent-retraining alternatives?

### Secondary

1. Does independent fresh verification add value over direct synthesis of the same committed reproductions?
2. Does plurality add value over one independent reproduction under the declared attacker and heterogeneity conditions?
3. Does proposal assistance reduce wasted launches/attempts enough to justify its added pathway?

These are component-necessity questions. A null or smaller-than-expected result removes or simplifies that component; it does not invalidate a measured primary result.

### Exploratory

- Which device proxies and baseline conditions respond, and how do support and role-window shift relate to response?
- How do declared poison, Byzantine-count, evidence-scarcity, contract-granularity, and heterogeneity levels change the boundary?
- What is the measured post-evidence runtime and communication cost?
- Does the mechanism direction persist on CICIoT2023 under its stated synthetic partition?

### Diagnostics

Shared label error, common spurious features, attacker-induced context, source-copy reproduction, report-time completeness, calibration/threshold leakage, and capture/role identity checks diagnose assumptions. They should bound or qualify the primary claim, not become unrelated headline claims.

The current roadmap names 19 claim states and plans 1,989 cells. Keep that registry as the prospective evidence inventory, but do not require every exploratory boundary to succeed before reporting a valid primary null, conditional effect, or mechanism simplification. The reporting gate should require complete primary evidence and render untested/undefined secondary claims as `Not Tested`/`NA`; otherwise a large unrelated diagnostic inventory can veto the central result.

## Claims, comparisons, endpoints, and gates

1. **Primary claim:** conditional evidence about malicious-authority exclusion and target capability retention within the tested source, attacker, dataset, and device-proxy envelope. Do not claim truth, benign origin, causal correctness, organizational independence, or general deployment security.
2. **Required comparisons:** source/model approval or client review; one independent retrain with the same contract/gate; multiple retrains with direct Krum; and the FedSIRA path with equal reproduction opportunities. Compare direct Krum against the same committed rows to isolate verification value. Retain local/centralized/FedAvg references only where they answer the capability-retention or FL-value question.
3. **Required controls:** matched clean source and useful-backdoored source; source-copy attack; a negative control for irrelevant proposals; fixed split/seed pairing; fresh verification/final-gate roles; and equal compute/evidence budgets for component necessity.
4. **Required ablations:** proposal opening, plurality, and external verification only if the corresponding preregistered necessity test is the claim. Avoid a general ablation zoo. A random grouping/control is warranted only if a grouping or domain structure is used to claim mechanism value.
5. **Primary metrics:** malicious admission rate and attack success for compromised production authority; legitimate admission and target F1 for availability; supported Macro-F1 drop and benign false-alarm-rate increase for clean cost; domain-level lower tail/worst-domain target F1 for harm concentration. Report denominators and undefined values. Accuracy/AUROC are secondary diagnostics unless a claim depends on them.
6. **Statistics:** paired seed-level effects, confidence intervals/effect sizes, exact registered tests, and multiplicity over the declared confirmatory family. Ten seeds with a nine-pair minimum may detect only large effects; no power/variance estimate from claim-bearing runs exists yet. Do not expand or shrink seeds post hoc. A clearly labelled feasibility pilot should estimate variance before protocol lock if the planned effects are near the decision margins.
7. **Hard gates:** identity/role isolation, no source artifact in production inputs, immutable contract, commit-before-verifier assignment, required sample support, Krum's mathematical row-count condition, fresh final gate, and no report/test selection leakage.
8. **Evidence/materiality gates:** keep validity gates strict. Treat null effects, heterogeneous responses, and effects smaller than the hoped-for threshold as results with intervals; do not convert them to `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`. Reserve that state for unresolved or technically incomplete inference. The configured materiality margins need a substantive rationale and should accompany continuous estimates rather than decide whether a valid cell exists.
9. **Feasibility:** the 9-device target support clears the configured minimum of 7, but role counts come from capture segments. The CIC schema/class census and pseudo-domains are known. Avoid launching predictable unsupported class/domain cells; distinguish eligible, undefined, insufficient, and not tested.
10. **External validation:** not required to make a narrowly bounded mechanism claim. It is required before claiming independent-site transportability; the current secondary synthetic pseudo-domains do not supply that validation. Highest-value addition would be a dataset with distinct capture/session provenance and the same target capability under a different collection context.

## Promoted decision

The specification, objective, source-authority constraint, pseudocode, complexity, invariants, and prior-art separation are in `docs/algorithms/leave-fault-geometric-median-certificate.md` and in Roadmap section 0. ## Baseline lifecycle formulation

Name: source-excluded post-reference admission lifecycle, retained as a comparison arm.

Scientific motivation. A participant may be the first to name a target-detection capability and may also be Byzantine. Approving that participant's artifact makes the adversary the production authority. Filtering submitted updates, which is what FedReview, FLShield, SureFED, FLTrust, and FedREDefense do, answers a different question: which ordinary round update to keep. The baseline asks whether a clean anchor, non-source local evidence, and a fresh gate can admit the capability while the proposal artifact has production weight 0. The certificate answers the same admission problem with a leave-`f` minimax utility and a geometric median of coalition means.

Inputs. Clean anchor parameters. One immutable capability contract. One source-domain identity. Ordered non-source reproduction updates. Verifier reports bound to commitment hashes. Fresh final-gate metrics on non-source domains. Declared bounds and thresholds from `configs/fedsira.yaml`: committee size 5, `f_R=1`, panel size 3, `f_V=1`, quorum 2, at least 6 adequate non-source final-gate domains, median target F1 at least 0.80, minimum domain target F1 at least 0.60, supported macro-F1 drop at most 0.02, benign false-alarm increase at most 0.01.

Outputs. One terminal or pending `AdmissionState`. On the synthesis path the production checkpoint is the anchor plus exactly one selected update. Admitted is reachable only when the final-gate predicates pass.

State. Commitments are written before verifier assignment. Certification counts positive reports. The synthesis committee is a map from domain identity to an update vector. Artifact identity does not include the payload bytes.

Assumptions. At most one Byzantine reproduction row in the committee and at most one Byzantine verifier in a panel. Honest rows are trained from the anchor on non-source evidence. Domains are device proxies. Roles are disjoint row-order windows of one capture. The proposal may be inspected as a proposal. On the honest path it is not a synthesis input.

Formulation. A row is certified when the positive-report count is at least `q = f_V + 1`. At the configured quorum the implied honest lower bound is `max(q - f_V, 0) = 1`. Krum is admissible only for `n >= 2 f_R + 3`. The score of a row is the sum of squared Euclidean distances to its `n - f_R - 2` nearest other rows. The selected row minimizes that score, with domain-id tie-break independent of input order. For `n=5` and `f_R=1` the neighbor count is 2. The source domain identity is forbidden in the synthesis row ids on Full FedSIRA and One Independent Retrain. The final gate admits only when every configured utility predicate passes and the adequate-domain count holds; too few adequate domains is Dormant; a failed predicate is Rejected.

Decision criterion. Admit a checkpoint iff it was synthesized without the source identity and the fresh final-gate predicates pass.

Constraints. Commitment timestamp before verifier assignment. One vote per domain. The source is not a verifier except on a named ablation. An inadmissible Krum committee does not synthesize. `report` does not train to fill missing evidence. Named ablations that insert the source delta are not the honest path: `use_source_delta_for_source_domain` and `force_first_row_to_source_delta` stay false there.

Mechanism. Fix the contract. Train non-source reproductions from the anchor. Commit each update. Assign the verifier panel from the commitment hash. Certify by the quorum. If plurality is active, run Krum on the committee; otherwise keep the single certified update. Add the selected update to the anchor. Score fresh final-gate roles that exclude the source. Apply the dormant / rejected / admitted transition.

Pseudocode.

```text
rows <- train_from_anchor(non_source_domains)
for row in rows:
    commit(row)
    assign_verifiers_after(commit)
    certify(row) if positives(row) >= f_V + 1
require source_identity not in synthesis_ids(certified)
update <- krum(certified) if plurality else the single certified row
checkpoint <- anchor + update
if adequate_non_source_final_gate_domains < 6: return Dormant
if utility_predicates(checkpoint): return Admitted
return Rejected
```

Complexity. Training dominates. Krum is `O(n^2 d)` with `n=5` (Blanchard et al., NeurIPS 2017). Panel assignment is a deterministic order of the eligible domains. The final gate evaluates each adequate non-source domain once. Communication stays the fixed message inventory already used for efficiency accounting. Storage on the honest path keeps the proposal artifact out of the synthesis input.

Determinism. Master seed and commitment hash drive assignment. Krum ties break by domain id.

Hyperparameters. Protocol, contract, and evidence thresholds live only in `configs/fedsira.yaml`. They are not chosen from outcomes.

Invariants. Source identity absent from the synthesis ids just produced. Direct production weight 0. A lone positive report does not certify. One dissenting report does not veto. Krum's neighbor count at the declared bound is 2. A far outlier is not the Krum selection when the other rows form a tight cluster. Admitted requires the final-gate predicates. A reported malicious-admission mean equals the binary admission rate of seed indicators that are exactly 0 or exactly 1. Any other seed value is rejected before that rate is computed. Under `One Byzantine Post-Reference Participant`, the clean legitimate source is not a compromised contributor. Admitting only that source scores malicious admission 0.

Failure modes. An in-ball non-source vector can win Krum while identity exclusion still holds. Exact payload equality is not an admission veto; `validate_production_checkpoint_excludes_source` is not given the source tensor on this path, so it does not compare payloads. A conformal risk target of 0.05 or 0.10 is not attainable from 6 or 8 calibration domains. Shared capture context remains. One Byzantine verifier can reduce the honest lower bound to 1, cannot certify a row alone, and cannot veto.

Why this baseline is not the contribution. Krum, the quorum, source-identity exclusion, and the utility thresholds are components with prior art. The promoted contribution is the Leave-Fault Geometric Median Certificate, which scores every leave-`f` coalition and deploys the geometric median of the coalition means.

Closest algorithms and the novelty boundary are in the candidate registry and in `Reviewer Audit.md`.

Expected measurable behavior. Against source review, one independent retrain with the same gate, and direct Krum on the same committed rows, the lifecycle should reduce compromised production admission while legitimate admission and target utility stay inside the preregistered margins. No claim-bearing estimate of that difference exists. The 1,989-cell grid was not run.

Required baselines. Client review with direct source admission. Client review then one independent retrain. One independent retrain. Direct Krum on the same reproduction rows. Coordinate-wise median only where the registry already names it.

Required ablations. Proposal opening, plurality, and external verification, each only as its preregistered necessity contrast. Named negative controls: no origin exclusion, Byzantine source-copy, and no final gate. No general ablation zoo.

POCs. Quorum enumeration, Krum outlier versus in-ball selection, conformal finite-sample floor, one-capture nearest-role distance, and the scaler fit predicate. All are non-confirmatory. See `POC Results.md`.

Production mapping. `fedsira run` reaches `ProtocolCellDispatch._advance_protocol`, then `reproduction_progression` or `single_verifier_progression`, then `reproduction_row_is_certified`, then `final_gate_decision` and `select_krum_update`, then `final_gate_predicates_pass` and `synthesis_pending_transition`. The source-exclusion cell calls `require_source_identity_excluded_from_synthesis` on `_last_synthesis_row_ids`. The honest synthesis call keeps both source-delta flags false. Ordinary-client model replacement is selected by `ProtocolCellExecutor._model_replacement_training_args` and applied inside the FedAvg, Krum, density, reconstruction, secure-continual, and certified-ensemble trainers. Malicious admission is `malicious_admission_from_ancestry` on the contributor ids those trainers accept. It is not the method name. A client who is designated but not accepted has malicious admission 0 when the cell is admitted. The reported seed-level mean is `malicious_admission_rate` on indicators that are exactly 0 or exactly 1. `_aggregate_rows` rejects every other seed value before calling that rate, including a compensating pair such as 0.5 and 1.5. The helper returns `AdmissionIndicator` values. A raw `bool` return is a primitive production boundary. Under `One Byzantine Post-Reference Participant`, source admission, sanitization, recovery without rollback, and independent local reference keep the clean legitimate source. Their malicious admission is 0 when that source is the only accepted contributor. `Useful Backdoored Source — 5%` still treats that source as compromised authority.

## Candidate registry

Each candidate was compared with the current lifecycle. The promoted status belongs to the Leave-Fault Geometric Median Certificate. Rows below it record rejections. The earlier lifecycle-only stop is withdrawn.

### Leave-Fault Geometric Median Certificate

Problem: post-reference capability admission when the proposing source must have production weight 0 and up to `f` independent evidence domains may be Byzantine. Mechanism: `J*` is the minimum leave-`f` lower-median utility, `H*` is the maximum leave-`f` upper-median harm, and the production update is the Weiszfeld geometric median of the leave-`f` coalition means. Decision: `PROMOTED`. Specification: `docs/algorithms/leave-fault-geometric-median-certificate.md`. The executed comparison is `docs/audit/pocs/leave_fault_candidates.json`.

### Source-excluded admission lifecycle

Problem: post-reference capability admission under one Byzantine reproducer and one Byzantine verifier. Mechanism: the baseline formulation above. Role: comparison arm, not the contribution. Feasibility: implemented and still executable. POC: on the controlled worlds the lifecycle installs the in-geometry malicious vector and rejects the single-low useful panel; the certificate does neither, at the same `f`, utility floor, and harm ceiling. Decision: baseline. The alternatives that remain rejected below lose on a guarantee this sample size cannot support, on a veto, on making a preregistered contrast true by construction, or on collapsing to identity exclusion only when a stored weight is exact.

### Split-conformal or conformal-risk final gate

Problem: fixed utility thresholds have no finite-sample risk guarantee. Mechanism: conformal risk control, `E[L] <= alpha` via `(sum L_i + B) / (n + 1)` (Angelopoulos, Bates, et al., arXiv 2208.02814), or a split-conformal rank of `ceil((n+1)(1-alpha)) / n`. Closest prior art: that paper, selective classification (Geifman and El-Yaniv, 2017), and SCoRE (Bai and Jin, arXiv 2603.24704). POC: at 6 adequate domains the finite-sample floor is `1/7 ≈ 0.143`; at 8 non-source domains it is `1/9 ≈ 0.111`. For alpha 0.05 and 0.10 the floor exceeds alpha even when every calibration loss is 0, and the split-conformal rank fraction is above 1. Alpha 0.20 is the first of the three probed levels that a perfect calibrator can meet, and the rank fraction is then 1, so only a threshold beyond every calibration score would be valid. Feasibility: the device-proxy count cannot host the guarantee the literature uses to justify the method. Decision: `REJECTED`. The occupied rule would abstain or become vacuous at the study's domain count. It is not rejected because it would be work to implement.

### Unanimity, or an honest lower bound of 2

Problem: quorum 2 with `f_V=1` certifies a row that may contain only one honest positive. Mechanism: require three positive reports, equivalently `observed - f_V >= 2`. POC, using `reproduction_row_is_certified` and `minimum_honest_positive_count`: panel 3, quorum 2, one dissent certifies, a lone positive does not, and the same one-dissent panel fails if the required count is the full panel. The honest lower bound at the shipped quorum is 1. Decision: `REJECTED`. On this panel the alternative is a Byzantine veto. The shipped quorum is the unique count that both rejects a lone positive and still certifies when one verifier dissents.

### Geometric veto of a source payload inside Krum

Problem: identity exclusion does not see a non-source row that carries the source vector. Mechanism: reject the Krum winner when its vector equals, or is within a tolerance of, the submitted source artifact. POC, using `select_krum_update`: with `f_R=1` and five rows, a value of 50 against a tight cluster at 0 to 0.2 is not selected; the center value 4.5 among 0, 3, 6, and 9 is selected. Identity exclusion still returns true when the source id is a domain outside that committee, and returns false when the selected row's own id is passed as the source. Closest prior art: Krum itself for the outlier; Baruch et al. (arXiv 1902.06156) and El Mhamdi et al. (ICML 2018) for in-ball evasion; FLTrust for cosine-to-a-trusted-reference, which is not a veto against the untrusted submission. Decision: `REJECTED`. An equality veto would make the preregistered source-copy contrast true by construction and would hide whether the utility predicates admit a backdoor. An in-ball mimic is a known Krum limitation and stays a claim boundary.

### SpectralKrum or bucketing in place of Krum

Problem: Krum's cluster assumption fails when honest updates are dispersed, which the in-ball POC illustrates. Mechanism: project updates to a historical spectral subspace and run Krum there (SpectralKrum, arXiv 2512.11760), or average random buckets before a robust aggregator (Karimireddy, He, and Jaggi, arXiv 2006.09365). Novelty: both are published robust-aggregation methods. They do not construct a new capability from a clean anchor. A vector sketch can show Krum's failure mode; it cannot show that either replacement improves malicious admission on this protocol without training. Decision: `REJECTED`. Promoting one would swap the declared operator for occupied prior art and change the question. Residual limitation: do not cite Krum's convergence theorem as a guarantee under device heterogeneity. That replacement is `FUTURE_INDEPENDENT_RESEARCH` if a later study asks a robust-aggregation question. It is not a missing piece of this admission study.

### SureFED uncertainty inspection

Problem: geometric rules miss stealthy poisoning. Mechanism: each client trains a clean Bayesian local reference and scores incoming updates by uncertainty (SureFED, arXiv 2308.02747). The scored object is the submitted update. FedSIRA's premise is that the submitted source artifact is not the authority to be scored into production. The current trainer has no uncertainty head, so a performance POC would be claim-bearing training rather than a cheap probe. Decision: `REJECTED` because the decision object is already occupied and is not the admission object. No numeric SureFED-versus-lifecycle result is claimed.

(6) Assumption: the clean reference is honest and the scored object is the submitted update. ### Historical subtraction of a retained source update

1. Problem being solved. Remove a source client's influence from an already averaged checkpoint without rebuilding that checkpoint from a clean anchor.
2. Failure mode of the current approach. Identity exclusion never puts the source identity in the synthesis inputs, so it has nothing to subtract. A reviewer can still ask whether subtraction would erase a source that averaging had already mixed in.
3. Inputs. An aggregate vector, the retained source update, and a scalar weight.
4. Outputs. A corrected vector.
5. State. The server must have stored the source update and the weight that was applied.
6. Assumptions. The production map is linear in that update, and the stored weight equals the weight that entered the average. A later nonlinear step breaks the equality.
7. Mathematical formulation. `corrected = aggregate - guessed_weight * source`. The identity-excluded vector is the aggregate built without the source term. With aggregate `= honest + true_weight * source`, the residual is `(true_weight - guessed_weight) * source`.
8. Objective. Match the identity-excluded vector.
9. Constraints. The stored update and the applied weight are the ones that entered. The honest FedSIRA path does not store the source update as a synthesis input.
10. Mechanism. Store the historical update. Form the average. Subtract the guessed source term.
11. Pseudocode. `corrected = (honest + true_weight * source) - guessed_weight * source`.
12. Complexity. One vector axpy, linear in the parameter count, plus storage of the retained update.
13. Communication and storage. The server keeps a client update that the honest synthesis path is required not to treat as production authority.
14. Determinism. Exact arithmetic on the stored vectors is deterministic. A wrong stored weight is a deterministic error.
15. Hyperparameters. The subtracted weight. The probe uses true weight 0.2 and guessed weight 0.05, and repeats the exact-weight case after scaling the source payload by 50.
16. Invariant. At the exact weight, `aggregate - true_weight * source` equals the honest vector, including after that payload scaling.
17. Failure modes. A wrong weight leaves the source component. The probe residual L2 is 0.9246621131896973, equal to the L2 of `(0.2 - 0.05) * source`. Identity exclusion of the scaled payload returns the honest vector.
18. Why this is not a rename. Subtraction is an unlearning operator on a historical aggregate. Identity exclusion is a membership constraint on synthesis inputs. They coincide only in the exact-weight linear case.
19. Closest algorithms. FedEraser reconstructs an unlearned model from retained historical client updates (Liu, Ma, Yang, Wang, and Liu, arXiv 2012.13891). FedRecovery subtracts a weighted sum of gradient residuals.
20. Exact novelty relative to them. Those methods already occupy client-level removal from an aggregate that included the client. The honest FedSIRA path never includes the source identity, so that removal problem does not arise there.
21. Expected measurable behavior. Exact-weight subtraction matches identity exclusion. Any weight error leaves a source component proportional to the error and to the payload magnitude. A scaled model-replacement payload therefore leaves a scaled residual when the weight is wrong.
22. Required baselines. Identity exclusion, exact-weight subtraction, and wrong-weight subtraction.
23. Required ablations. Payload scale 1 versus 50 at the same true weight. Both are in the probe.
24. Required POC. `docs/audit/pocs/unlearning_residual_poc.py`, summarized in `POC Results.md`. All four checks passed: the residual matches the weight-error term, exact-weight subtraction matches identity exclusion, identity exclusion is invariant to the 50-times payload, and the wrong weight leaves a source component. Non-confirmatory. Not a grid arm.
25. Production implementation implications. Do not add a subtraction arm and do not store source updates for later removal on the honest path. Keep `require_source_identity_excluded_from_synthesis`.

Decision: `REJECTED`. The operator does not improve the admission rule. It is not rejected because it would be work to implement. The vector identity already shows it is equal to the current rule only at the exact weight and strictly weaker otherwise.

### Reputation-weighted or attested aggregation of ongoing clients

1. Problem being solved. Down-weight or exclude clients whose recent updates, devices, or code images look malicious, while still training by repeated aggregation.
2. Failure mode of the current approach. The lifecycle does not maintain a reputation, a TPM quote, or a TEE measurement, so it cannot express "this client's history should change its aggregation weight."
3. Inputs. A stream of client updates, plus reputation, attestation, or explanation features.
4. Outputs. A weighted or filtered aggregate for the next round.
5. State. Reputation, attestation history, or a trust score.
6. Assumptions. Ongoing clients remain the production contributors. Historical behavior predicts the next update. Attestation binds the code image that produced the update.
7. Mathematical formulation. FedRISC-IIoT maps historical behavior to participation weights and then applies global filtering, coordinate pruning, and reputation-weighted fusion (Future Generation Computer Systems 185, 108640, 2026). HPoT uses a hierarchical proof-of-trust and tolerates up to floor(N/3) Byzantine nodes (Chaurasia, Sharma, and Rathore, Scientific Reports 16, 21929, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41598-026-49902-4). ZTA-FL attests with a TPM and aggregates with SHAP weights (arXiv 2512.23809). Sentinel attests local training inside a TEE and drops a client whose attestation fails for that round (Zhang et al., arXiv 2509.00634).
8. Objective. Reduce poisoning success among the clients who still enter the average.
9. Constraints. The decision object is the next client update. None of these rules construct a new capability from a clean anchor after giving the proposal artifact weight 0.
10. Mechanism. Score or attest the client, change the weight or the membership, aggregate the survivors.
11. Pseudocode. `aggregate(weight(client) * update(client) for client in attested_or_reputable(clients))`.
12. Complexity. Reputation is linear in clients and rounds. Attestation adds a quote verification per round. SHAP weighting adds an explanation pass. All sit on top of ordinary aggregation.
13. Communication and storage. Extra quotes, trust scores, or explanation payloads per round. FedSIRA's honest path does not add that channel.
14. Determinism. A deterministic score is deterministic. Remote attestation depends on the device quote.
15. Hyperparameters. Reputation decay, attestation policy, SHAP or pruning thresholds. None are in `configs/fedsira.yaml` because the rule is not the study's rule.
16. Invariants. A failed attestation or a low reputation can remove a client from one round. That is not the invariant "source identity is absent from synthesis ids."
17. Failure modes. A client who passes attestation can still submit a poisoned update. A reputation score can punish a shifted honest device. Hierarchical trust can concentrate authority in a high-tier node. None of these failures is the source-admission failure the lifecycle is built to measure.
18. Why this is not a rename. Each paper already names a filter, a weight, or an attestation of ongoing updates. Composing them with Krum would still be update filtering.
19. Closest algorithms. FedRISC-IIoT, HPoT, ZTA-FL, and Sentinel, as cited above. FedDynamic (Journal of Cloud Computing, 2026, doi 10.1186/s13677-026-00993-4) is the same neighborhood: fingerprint, watermark, and trust-weighted aggregation.
20. Exact novelty relative to them. They occupy selection and weighting of clients who continue to train the global model. The unresolved object is one-shot admission of a named capability whose proposal artifact has production weight 0, with the checkpoint built from a clean anchor and non-source evidence.
21. Expected measurable behavior. These methods can lower poisoning among averaged clients. They do not, by their stated mechanisms, answer whether source review admits a compromised proposal that reconstruction would have excluded.
22. Required baselines. FedAvg and Krum, which the registry already runs as ordinary-FL references, plus the lifecycle. A new reputation arm is not required to identify the lifecycle.
23. Required ablations. Reputation on versus off, attestation on versus off. Those ablations belong to the papers that introduce the weights. They are not FedSIRA arms.
24. Required POC. No training probe. The separation is the decision object, which is visible from the papers' mechanisms and from the shipped identity-exclusion guard. A performance comparison would be claim-bearing training and was not run.
25. Production implementation implications. Do not add reputation state, a TPM quote, a TEE recorder, or SHAP weights to the honest path. Do not change the 1,989-cell registry.

Decision: `REJECTED`. The family is occupied prior art on a different object. It is not rejected because attestation would be hard to implement. Implementing it would change the question.

## Final scientific design

Contribution. The Leave-Fault Geometric Median Certificate, specified in `docs/algorithms/leave-fault-geometric-median-certificate.md` and Roadmap section 0. A post-reference source may name the capability. The certificate admits when the worst leave-`f` lower-median utility and the worst leave-`f` upper-median harm clear the configured floor and ceiling, and the production update is the geometric median of those coalition means. The source production weight is 0. The lifecycle is a baseline arm in that comparison. It is not the method.

Minimal questions. Primary: inside the declared fault bound, does the certificate admit a useful capability whose evidence survives every leave-`f` deletion, and does it refuse a production update that is a malicious in-geometry vector, relative to the lifecycle, direct source admission, quorum, and Krum. Secondary lifecycle questions stay baseline questions: fresh verification versus direct synthesis of the same commitments; plurality versus one reproduction; proposal assistance versus the wasted launches it avoids. Exploratory boundaries stay exploratory.

Hypotheses. The primary contrast is two-sided in the data and one-sided only where the registry already says non-inferiority for utility. A null is a result. Component necessity is claimed only when its own preregistered family supports it.

Populations and units. N-BaIoT: 115 predictors, 11 classes, 9 device-domain proxies, target present in all 9, roles are row-order windows of one capture per device and class. CICIoT2023: 39 predictors, 31 canonical classes, 9 label-conditioned hash pseudo-domains, secondary direction only. The inferential unit is the paired master seed. Device proxies are strata. Rows, verifier reports, and client repetitions are not extra independent seeds. Ten seeds and a nine-pair minimum are the preregistered replication plan. No confirmatory variance estimate exists, so power is not claimed.

Datasets can prove a controlled device-proxy replay contrast under the feature-space threat. They cannot prove organizational independence, natural time, raw-traffic trigger realizability, privacy, or deployment readiness.

Baselines and controls. The lifecycle, direct source admission, quorum, Krum selection, the coordinate-wise median, and the adapted Bulyan comparator, on the same controlled evidence. The lifecycle's screen, quorum, source-identity exclusion, and final gate stay inside that baseline arm.

Ablations. Remove one certificate mechanism at a time: source exclusion, the worst-case fault bound, the geometric median, and the lower median. The in-geometry panel is the case that isolates the geometric median. The single-low panel and the sharp quality gap isolate the lower median.

Metrics. Malicious admission and attack success for compromised authority. Legitimate admission and target F1 for availability. Supported macro-F1 harm and benign false-alarm increase for clean cost. Worst-domain target F1 for harm concentration. Denominators stay explicit. Undefined stays undefined.

Statistics. Paired seed-level effects, confidence intervals, the registered tests, and multiplicity inside the declared confirmatory family. Heterogeneity Boundary uses the Section 35 absolute-degradation rule on resolved paired means, not a Holm-only predicate. Other claims that the roadmap ties to Holm still use that predicate. Empty evidence stays Not Tested. `report` fails closed.

Gates. Identity and role isolation, source identity absent from honest synthesis ids, immutable contract, commit-before-assignment, sample-support minima, Krum's row-count condition, fresh final gate, no test-informed threshold. Materiality margins do not delete a valid null.

Feasibility. Nine target-holding devices clear the minimum of 7. Role support is capture segments, not independent replications. The confirmatory grid is an external resource boundary and was not started.

Claim boundaries. No distribution-free admission risk at alpha 0.05 or 0.10 on 6 to 8 domains. No claim that Krum rejects an in-ball payload copy. The certificate's production update on that panel is a geometric median of coalition means, which is a different object from a claim that Krum itself rejects the copy. No claim that quorum 2 implies two honest positives. No claim that subtracting a stored source update dominates identity exclusion: the two agree only at the exact linear weight, and a wrong weight leaves a residual proportional to the payload. No claim that reputation weights, TPM or TEE attestation, or SHAP-weighted aggregation are the certificate. No organizational, temporal, raw-traffic, privacy, above-bound security, or priority claim. At `n = 7` and `f = 1`, two low reports still leave lower-median index 2 at the high utility; the certificate does not treat every low report that exceeds `f` as an automatic rejection.
